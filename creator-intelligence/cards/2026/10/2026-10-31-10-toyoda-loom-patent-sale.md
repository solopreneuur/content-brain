# A loom patent paid for Toyota

**The find:** Toyota began as a loom company. In 1929 Sakichi Toyoda sold the rights to his automatic loom, which stopped itself the moment a thread broke, to Britain's Platt Brothers for £100,000, and passed the money to his son Kiichiro to research cars.
**The surprise:** A British textile-machinery firm, in an industry Toyota would later leave behind, helped bankroll Japan's biggest carmaker.
**The idea:** [Jidoka](../../../concepts/jidoka.md), "automation with a human touch": a machine that stops itself when something goes wrong, so one worker can watch many. The same idea became a core rule of the Toyota Production System.
