# 4/10 Broken window theory

Have you ever heard of the broken window theory?

In the 1990s, New York City was a complete shithole.

The streets were filthy, prostitution was everywhere, and crime was through the roof.

Then in 1993, Rudy Giuliani gets elected as mayor, and he brings in this new police commissioner named William Bratton,

and everyone's expecting this huge crackdown where cops are just gonna start arresting everyone.

That's not what happened. Instead, they do something no one expects. They clean up the subways.

They start fixing the broken windows on every car and cracking down on fare evasions.

Now everyone's criticizing them. You've got murders happening on the streets every night, and you're worried about the subway?

But these guys understood something about human psychology that no one else did, the broken window theory.

They knew that one broken window left unrepaired sends a signal. It tells everyone around, "No one's in charge here, and the standard is low.

Do whatever the hell you want." And we all have broken windows in our lives, whether it's the bed we left undone or the dishes in the sink.

These little things are telling your brain, "The standard here is low." But what happens when you change the signal?

After cleaning up the subway, violent crime in New York City dropped 56%, and it became one of the biggest urban crime turnarounds in American history.
