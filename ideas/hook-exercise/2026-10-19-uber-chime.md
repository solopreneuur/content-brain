**HOOK: sensory object** You ever been in an Uber and you hear this at the end of your ride?

**[STAKES: business intrigue + personal payoff]** Believe it or not, that little noise has been one of Uber's most profitable decisions, and the psychology behind it will help you build better habits.

**[PROBLEM]** See, Uber had a big problem. Their drivers would quit after just a handful of rides, like they just couldn't keep drivers on the platform, and it was costing them a ton of money.

**[FIX: the sound is the mechanism]** So geniusly, Uber turned to psychology to fix the issue. At the end of each ride, Uber automatically matches the driver to their next ride, hence the little chime noise.

**[CONCEPT: why it works]** What this does is it removes all friction between drivers and the act of driving for Uber. And studies prove that this removal of friction is the best way to reinforce our own habits.

**[APPLICATION: two viewer examples]** You wanna go on morning runs? Start sleeping in your running clothes. You wanna read more? Keep your phone in the kitchen and fill your room with books.

**[REFRAME: closing line, no CTA]** Because odds are you don't lack willpower, your environment just has way too much friction

Hook:
How expensive does this car sound?

Believe it or not, that little thunk is one of Mercedes' most profitable decisions, and the psychology behind it decides how seriously you're taken by people.

See Mercedes had a big problem, in 1973 the US government passed a bill saying car makers had to add extra bars to the side doors to meet safety regulations.

This made car doors sound cheap, so Mercedes dumped money into audio engineering and added precise foam padding to recreate the sound their older cars made.

Mercedes understood that if a car door sounds cheap, a buyer is much more likely to walk out.

This is called the halo effect and studies prove that one strong positive impression influences someones judgment of you more than everything else.

You wanna impress your investors? Wear a strong old money cologne. You wanna be respected when you walk into a room with high achievers? Dress better than everyone there.

Because odds are you aren't less capable than them, your first impression just isn't telling them that.
